Amsterdam

Rebellion Meets Reinvention.

Amsterdam’s street art connects local subcultures with international exchange. Its landscape spans unauthorized tags, neighborhood murals, playful interventions, and museums dedicated to preserving the movement’s history.

Late 1970s —

Punk leaves its mark

Poet and graffiti pioneer Diana Ozon links writing on walls with Amsterdam’s punk scene, establishing a local culture of public expression.

–1980s

Local writing meets New York

Writer ZAP becomes known for tagging the city’s trams. In 1984, Henry Chalfant’s photographs of New York graffiti arrive in a gallery show, linking the two scenes.

–2000s

Industrial spaces become destinations

Large murals and international projects gain visibility around former industrial areas such as NDSM Wharf, shaping how these spaces are experienced.

2012–2020s

The movement finds a home

Street Art Museum Amsterdam begins in Nieuw-West, using public art to connect local histories and migration. In 2020, STRAAT Museum opens in a former NDSM warehouse.
